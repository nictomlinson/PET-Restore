# Commodore PET Restoration Progress

# 2026/10/29

Up early before the builders arrived since we are currently having building work
done around the house. Took some photos of the outside of the PET, opened up the
hood and photographed the inside. Apart from dust and a single germinating seed
the inside was particularly clean and tidy. The hood screws where not a matching
pair with one a hex head head and the one on the other side and a slotted screw
on the other but beyond that there was no evidence that anyone had ever been in
here before. No visible modifications. Brushed off some of the dust inside
and outside the case. and moved it into the workshop.

The first thing that struck me was that it did not have the wider 6550 RAM chips
that came with the early version of the PET. This PET has ceramic TMS
4108-250DL-1 dynamic RAM with date codes of fifth week of 1980.

Images from this first look can be found in the
[gallery](gallery/20261029_First_Look).

Some basic details such as model, serial number etc from this first look and,
updated as and when, can be found in [basic details](./basicDetails.md).

## 2026/10/28

Collected the Commodore PET from an ex-colleague and friend. Arrived home after
dark around 6:30, so just placed the PET in the garage. It was dusty but not
too bad for a machine that had spent 20 years in a farmhouse loft. However, I
didn't fancy opening it up in the morning in the cabin/workshop in case mice or
others had taken a liking to it.

It is a version 1 PET with the built-in cassette deck and chiclet keyboard.

To find out why I particularly wanted a Commodore PET, and a version 1 at that,
see my [rationale](rationale.md).
