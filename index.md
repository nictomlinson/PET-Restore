# Commodore PET Restoration Progress

# 2026/11/02

I received a reply from the maintainer of
[PET Labels](http://cbmsteve.ca/petlabels/), Steve Gray yesterday regarding
the photo I sent him of the label on the PET. He said it looks like a new
variation for his website. It is similar to number 11 (as at 2026/11/02) but
with CBM instead of PET. He said he hasn't come across a new label in quite a
while.

Well, that is something noteworthy about this PET.

# 2026/10/31

I thought I would jot down some thoughts about how I might go about getting the
PET working. To start, I don't know that it does not work. I could just plug
it in and hope for the best. However, I am not that brave. So I think this is
roughly what I will do:

- disassemble the whole machine as best I can
- dump the ROM chips to check they read correctly
- clean everything
- visually inspect of the board, check diode drops and power rail shorts
- reform the main filter cap or replace it if it won't
- put enough back together to test it but with all the socketed chips removed
- plug it in while monitoring the power rail voltages
- if the power rails all look good, reseat all the chips, remembering to dexoit
the sockets, and see if she works
- and if she does, put it all back together and marvel at glorious 1980s tech

If all goes well and I have a working PET then I need to decide whether to
upgrade it at all or leave it stock. I am tending toward stock at the moment
given how fresh from the factory it currently looks. If I was to upgrade
it in anyway, then I think I would limit it to non-invasive changes that can
be invisibly reversed.

I also need to decide what to do with the damaged front
[label](./images/20261029_First_Look/firstLook001.JPG). Should I replace it with
a perfect looking replica, assuming I can get one, or leave the damage as part
of its history. Again, I am tending toward leaving as is.

# 2026/10/29

Up early before the builders arrived since we are currently having building work
done around the house. Took some photos of the outside of the PET, opened up the
hood and photographed the inside. Apart from dust and a single germinating seed
the inside was particularly clean and tidy. The hood screws where not a matching
pair with one a hex head head and the one on the other side and a slotted screw
on the other but beyond that there was no evidence that anyone had ever been in
here before. No visible modifications. Brushed off some of the dust inside
and outside the case. and moved it into the workshop.

The first thing that struck me was that it did not have the wider 6550 RAM chips
that came with the early version of the PET. This PET has ceramic TMS
4108-250DL-1 dynamic RAM with date codes of fifth week of 1980.

Images from this first look can be found in the
[gallery](gallery/20261029_First_Look).

Some basic details such as model, serial number etc from this first look and,
updated as and when, can be found in [basic details](./basicDetails.md).

## 2026/10/28

Collected the Commodore PET from an ex-colleague and friend. Arrived home after
dark around 6:30, so just placed the PET in the garage. It was dusty but not
too bad for a machine that had spent 20 years in a farmhouse loft. However, I
didn't fancy opening it up in the morning in the cabin/workshop in case mice or
others had taken a liking to it.

It is a version 1 PET with the built-in cassette deck and chiclet keyboard.

To find out why I particularly wanted a Commodore PET, and a version 1 at that,
see my [rationale](rationale.md).
